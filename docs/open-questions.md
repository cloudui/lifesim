# Open questions for the project owner

Big questions that came up while building, newest first. Each one says what was done in the meantime, so nothing is blocked. Delete an entry once it is decided, and record the decision in `AGENTS.md` or `docs/experience.md`.

## From the endings work (2026-11-12)

1. **Circles are now clearly too small.** Once friendships can actually end, the average person has about 26 friends and 58 acquaintances (targets 50 and 150; before endings it was 34 and 71). The fix is probably on the making side: people meet and keep too few casual friends. *In the meantime:* left alone, as the brief says not to tune until the structure settles. Is roughly-human circle size important enough to do a calibration pass soon?

2. **The world empties out.** Deaths follow real mortality, and nobody is born or arrives, so 59% of the 2,000 people die over 40 years. The protagonist's world ages with them, which is true to life, but there are no young colleagues or new neighbours late in life. *In the meantime:* nothing added. Options: young adults arrive each year; or children (which also adds a whole kind of relationship).

3. **The protagonist cannot die.** Everyone else can. A story that always runs 40 years is simpler to show, but it is the one rule that does not apply to everyone. *In the meantime:* exempt.

4. **How death is shown.** The wording is plain ("Your mom, Mei, died, aged 70."), a person's lane ends with a small open circle, and they leave the map and the walk. There is no scene for it. Is that the right tone, or should a death (at least of family or a partner) get a quiet moment of its own in the Walk?

5. **Family never fall out.** Only friends do. Estrangement from a parent or sibling is a real ending, and easy to add with the same rule. Wanted?

6. **What a falling-out is about.** It is a dice roll weighted by fit and by how long two people have known each other; the story says "you fell out" with no reason. Reasons would make it more human (money, a partner, a slight, politics) but need either invented flavour text or more modelled state. Flavour text is cheap; is invented detail acceptable, given "never rig, only curate"?

## From the people and what-if work (2026-11-12)

7. **One alternative life or many, for "what if".** One rerun is a vivid story but chaotic: a meeting delayed by a week can change who is close decades later, so it cannot honestly say "because of Axel". Many reruns give odds ("in 31 of 100 lives you still end up with Dmitri") but cost a lot of simulation.

8. **Lives start settled.** Most of each cast is already close when the story begins. See the "less settled start" entry in `docs/history.md` for what was tried.
